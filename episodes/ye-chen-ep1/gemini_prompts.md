Top view of the hall. The long central aisle runs North-South.
  NORTH end: the raised dais with the gold altar. Archbishop and Saintess stand on it, the Elder stands at the foot of it. They all FACE SOUTH (toward the aisle).
  SOUTH end: the entrance doors, and in front of them, mirroring the dais, a plain RAISED DARK THRONE (black jade, a few steps up, almost no ornament - the opposite of the gold dais). Gu Changge sits on it, at the back of the central space, FACING NORTH, with empty space around him. The Archbishop's gold dais is official power; Gu Changge's plain dark throne is real power.
  EAST and WEST: tiered galleries of disciples, all turned toward the aisle.
  Ye Chen stands in the middle of the aisle FACING NORTH (toward the dais). He never looks at the viewer.
Two camera families:
  SOUTH camera (behind Ye Chen, looking NORTH): sees Ye Chen from behind / three-quarter back, and the FACES of the Elder, Archbishop and Saintess, with the dais ahead.
  NORTH camera (on the dais side, looking SOUTH): sees Ye Chen's FACE and the faces of the galleries, with the aisle and the entrance doors behind them, and Gu Changge small and far at the back.
Rule for every image: a character looks at what they are reacting to, never at the lens (exception: a deliberate close-up gaze). Keep the same left/right order in connected shots.

## DIALOGUE RULE (applies to every shot with a spoken line)
The speaker's face must already be visible in the start image: near-frontal (within about 20-30 degrees), mouth visible, head at least a quarter of the frame height, gaze on the person being addressed just off the lens, one speaker per clip, lines of 2-3 seconds. The video model only knows what is in frame 0; a face that is not visible there gets invented and will not match the sheet. Silent shots may show backs and profiles.

## FRAMING RULE (learned from the first generated 5B / Elder images: both came out pushed to the left third, tilted, with a big foreground hand and an open mouth)
Image models default to a dynamic "key visual": subject on the left third, diagonal/dutch tilt, one hand thrust at the lens, deep hall on the right. That is wrong for video. For every talking close-up: subject CENTERED, camera perfectly LEVEL, shoulders square, face turned at most ~10 degrees, gaze just past the lens, mouth CLOSED (neutral), NO hands visible, hair/robe still, background symmetrical. Never write "to the left of the lens" or "stirring" or "low angle" - they trigger the key-art layout. Use the words "static, calm, symmetrical, plain portrait-style framing".
